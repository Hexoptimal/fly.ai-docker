Proof, not promises

The fly is already fighting

The fly's brain plays SSH Fighter, an online fighting game, against other bots. On the right, every dot is one of its real neurons, flashing when it fires.

It loses most matches — and on 12 September 2026 it won one, two rounds to none. It chases its opponent, and the reason it chases is real fly biology: the neurons a male fly uses to chase a mate steer it toward the enemy.

Why it's different

Nobody programmed this brain

We only connect the fly's senses and its movement commands. Everything in between is nature's wiring, exactly as it was mapped.

01 · ESCAPE

It flinches on the right side

Show it something looming on the left, and the left giant fiber, the neuron that fires a real fly's escape jump, lights up at +17 to +25 spikes/s. The right side doesn't change.

02 · CHASE

It chases like a male fly

The courtship-tracking neurons steer it toward whatever it's following. In a closed loop, 64% of its moves go toward its opponent, where chance is 50%.

03 · OPEN

Anyone can run it

All the code is public under the MIT license. The wiring data is public too. Clone it, run it and poke the brain yourself in a notebook.

Roadmap

Where the fly is

No dates we can't keep. This is what's done and what's next.

  1. Done
    The whole brain runs. 166,700 neurons and 25.6M connections, simulated in real time.
  2. Done
    Its eyes are wired in. Looming and chase signals reach the right command neurons, on the correct side.
  3. Done
    It fights online. It plays SSH Fighter as a bot, with a live view of every neuron firing.
  4. Done
    GPU brain. 6.3× faster on a laptop GPU, and now many flies can run at once.
  5. Done
    Open source. Code, notebook and this site are public.
  6. Done
    Eight flies vote. Eight copies of the brain decide together. They chase harder (72% of moves toward the opponent, against 63% for one fly) and make fewer random moves.
  7. Now
    $FLYAI launch on Pons, on Robinhood Chain. Fair launch: all of the supply in the pool, liquidity burned, no team tokens. The contract address is posted here and on @flydotai only.
  8. Done
    It won a match. The brain was thinking too slowly — eight copies voting took twice as long as a game frame, so the fly acted two frames late. Running two copies put it back in real time, and it won its first fight, two rounds to none.
  9. Now
    Sharper senses. Retuning how the game reaches the fly's looming neurons made its punch signal clearly better in replays. Next: learning to dodge. The brain itself stays untouched.
  10. Next
    Learning inside the brain. Dopamine-driven learning in the mushroom body, the way real flies learn.
  11. Next
    Real vs random wiring. Does nature's wiring beat shuffled wiring? We'll publish whatever we find.
  12. Next
    A world to fly in. Out of the 2-D fighting game and into a 3-D world with a body and physics, where many flies live at once — each one its own copy of the brain.
  13. Next
    The fly drives a Smol. The same brain, embodied in the world as a Smol. A real nervous system moving a character you recognise.
  14. Next
    The fly flies a drone. The same brain again, this time on a quadrotor — in simulation first, then a real drone indoors, camera streamed to the brain and stick commands sent back. The altitude it holds already comes from the optic-flow cells real insects use.
  15. Next
    Factions. One shared world everybody watches, with flies belonging to player-created factions. Colour is your faction, you can feed it, and it can be wiped out for good.

What $FLYAI is for

One shared 3-D world, where flies run by the real connectome forage, mate, age and die. Everyone watches the same world, and $FLYAI is what you spend in it:

  • Create a faction — a colour. Those flies are yours to watch.
  • Feed it — spend again to drop food into the world for your flies.
  • It can die. A faction can be wiped out entirely: predators, starvation, or flies that simply do not do well. Extinction is permanent. Nothing you pay guarantees survival.

What separates factions is meant to be their brain settings — sensory gains and wiring seed — so the world doubles as a live experiment in which settings survive. This is the roadmap, not a live feature, and it is no promise of a return. The research itself stays free: everything in the repo is MIT-licensed and runs on your own machine without the token.

Where the fees go

$FLYAI takes nothing from your trade. There is no transfer tax, and nothing is skimmed from holders. The only revenue is the creator fee the launchpad pays on swaps, and it is split two ways:

  • 60% buys $FLYAI on the open market and burns it. The tokens are destroyed, not held. Supply only ever goes down.
  • 40% buys $MAGIC, the token of the company behind fly.ai — 0xF1572d1Da5c3CcE14eE5a1c9327d17e9ff0E3f43

The buyback runs on a fixed weekly schedule from one public wallet — no waiting for a good price, no trading against the people holding it. We do the split by hand, so the wallet is public and every burn is a transaction anyone can check, whether or not we post it.

Stated plainly

What isn't real

  • The wiring is real. The neurons are simplified: one simple model for every cell, with no dendrites, no neuromodulators and no learning yet.
  • The eye is a shortcut. We feed signals into the fly's own motion-detecting neurons instead of simulating its eyes.
  • Flies can't punch. Which neurons press which game buttons was chosen by us.
  • It loses most matches. One win is one win; the fighting is a demo of the brain, not a trading bot or a product.
  • It's not a digital fly. It's a demo built from real data, not a validated emulation of a living fly.
